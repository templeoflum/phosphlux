# LZX Cadet IV Dual Ramp Generator: what the circuit does, from its documents (T-0288)

A reading of LZX's own Cadet IV documents, stage by stage, with the source for every statement. No app code
and no simulation are involved. The simulation is in `README.md` (T-0278), and this page does not use its results.

Every statement carries one of these labels:

- **stated**: the value or fact is printed in the cited document. Open the citation and it is there.
- **datasheet**: a value from the manufacturer's datasheet for a part the BOM names. It is not in any LZX document.
- **derived**: arithmetic on stated (or datasheet) part values. The arithmetic is shown, so a checker can redo it.
- **community**: said by a forum user. Not maker documentation.
- **not stated**: looked for in the sources below and not found.

**What "derived" assumes.** Unless a line says otherwise, a derived number is a *nominal, idealised* figure. It
uses nominal part values (no tolerance) and the typical TL431 reference voltage (2.495 V, see TI below). It treats the
op-amps as ideal (no offset, bias current or gain error) and the rectifier diodes as ideal switches. It treats the current
source as ideal (no base-current loss, no output-resistance effect), the outputs as unloaded, and the reset as instant and
complete, down to 0 V. None of these idealisations is stated by LZX. A derived number describes the drawn circuit under
these assumptions, not a guaranteed range for the documented hardware.

## The module as a whole

- 4 HP Eurorack module, 65 mm deep, drawing 48 mA from +12 V and 29 mA from −12 V. Legacy status. Its "Video sync" column reads "16-pin Eurorack bus" (**stated**, SPEC line 89). Note: the schematic takes sync from the 14-pin header J1, not from the 16-pin bus (SCH 2/2 C4, and see stage 2).
- It has two identical channels: horizontal (sheet 1) and vertical (sheet 2). Each channel turns a sync pulse into a rising ramp, a falling ramp and a "V"-shaped triangle (**stated** circuit, SCH 1/2 and 2/2). A forum user also describes them as "basically separate and essentially identical" (**community**, F2190 post 3).
- **No front-panel controls.** The panel has six jacks and nothing else (**stated**, FPN). The BOM lists no potentiometers apart from the two trimmers R1 and R3 (**stated**, BOM line 29).
- **No inputs on the panel.** The module's only signal input is sync from the J1 header (**stated**, SCH 2/2 C4; FPN).

### Timing: reset and blanking

- **Reset:** the capacitor is shorted while `_HSYNC` [`_VSYNC`] is high (**stated**, SCH). Which part of the line that is depends on J1's polarity, and that is **not stated** in the Cadet IV documents (see stage 2a). If the header is active-low, as C1 p13 suggests, then each ramp is held at 0 for the sync pulse width and rises from the end of sync to the next sync (**derived, conditional**).
- **Ramp duration and peak:** the ramp rises for one period minus the reset time. H period = 1/15,734 Hz = 63.56 µs (NTSC) and 64.00 µs (PAL). V field = 1/59.97 Hz = 16.675 ms (NTSC) and 20.00 ms (PAL) (C1 p6). The sync widths are **not stated**, so the derivation can only use the full period. That gives a **conditional nominal zero-reset ceiling**, not a bound on real hardware. It assumes nominal capacitors, the ideal 1.00 mA current, a reset that takes no time and leaves 0 V, and the trimmer at 1.0. On those terms, H: 17.86 mV/µs × 63.56 µs = **1.135 V** (NTSC) or 1.143 V (PAL); V: 66.7 mV/ms × 16.675 ms = **1.112 V** (NTSC) or **1.333 V** (PAL). The stated capacitor tolerances alone move these a long way. C29 at −10 % / +10 % gives an H NTSC ceiling of 1.261 / 1.032 V. C18 at −20 % / +20 % gives a V NTSC ceiling of 1.390 / 0.926 V, and V PAL 1.667 / 1.111 V. Any reset time shortens the rise, and any residual voltage raises the floor; both are unknown. Under the same assumptions, the V ramp needs a different trimmer setting for each standard and the H ramp nearly does not (**derived, conditional**).
- **Blanking:** the Cadet IV does not use the Blanking pin (pin 3 not connected, SCH 2/2 C4). Nothing in the circuit stops the outputs during blanking intervals: each ramp keeps rising through horizontal and vertical blanking until its reset (**derived** from the schematic). Whether downstream modules blank it is **not stated** here.
- **Interlace:** the Cadet I is 480i/576i (C1 p5). Whether alternate fields differ in length at the V reset (half-line offset) is **not stated**.

gives Q4, Q7, Q8, Q9, Q12 and Q14. Adding the TL431 datasheet also gives Q3 and the Q10 value. All of these are

and edge, noise and spread (Q16) have no value in any source read.** A measured unit or an LZX scope trace would be needed for those.

## How this page was checked

- Both schematic sheets were rendered at 200 dpi, and the triangle stage at 400 dpi, then read by eye. Every part value in the table was checked against the image and the BOM.
- The PCB drawing and front-panel artwork were fetched from lzxcadet and read by eye. All three forum threads were fetched as Discourse JSON and every post was read in full. The Cadet I PDF text was extracted with `pdftotext` and its pages were located by form-feed index.
- Derived values were worked by hand. The triangle relation was first taken from T-0278 and then rechecked here, because a quick reading that leaves out the R20/R10 loading wrongly gives unequal gains for the two halves.
- Correction pass (after the verifier's findings): the TI TL431 datasheet was fetched and its text extracted. The §6.13 TL431BQ table on page 14 supplies Vref, Iref and ΔVref/ΔVKA. Tolerance and ceiling numbers were recomputed with a short Python calculation.
- Fetched copies (PCB, panel, photo, C1, threads, TI datasheet) are in `.scratch/t0288/` (git-ignored) and were not committed to the repo.
